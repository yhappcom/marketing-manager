## Latest validated addition — 124
Canonical principle: **A collapsible or larger banner is not free revenue; expansion consumes an explicit intrusion budget.** Google documents anchored adaptive banners as persistent top/bottom layout inventory with stable returned height, inline adaptive banners for scrollable content, and collapsible banners as an initially larger overlay. Google also deliberately suppresses repeat collapsible requests during auto-refresh because repeated expansion can harm user experience. Banner proximity to interactive controls is a major accidental-click risk, and Confirmed Click can be applied when click quality deteriorates.

AP3 requires a natural persistent surface, appropriate adaptive geometry, protected-control separation, stable load/fail/refresh/orientation/accessibility layout, test-ad QA, explicit eligible-visible denominator, U-compatible paid-event measurement, B/E downstream guardrails, privacy/age compatibility, and owned lifecycle. AP3-C adds a separate justification for collapsible expansion: incremental retained-user economics must plausibly exceed obstruction cost, protected task state cannot be covered, repeat expansion must be bounded, and normal-banner/mediation fallback must remain acceptable. A visible collapse control does not cancel the initial interruption.

MintTap must protect transaction/portfolio/ticker/ROC-tax/distribution/calculation/warning/chart interactions. LogMate must protect flight entry/save/edit/delete, totals/search, import/duplicate review, backup/restore/offline/recovery and operational warnings. Large/collapsible banners are never automatic upgrades from a stable anchored placement.
