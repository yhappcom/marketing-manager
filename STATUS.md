## Latest validated addition — 203
Google Play Store Listing experiments are not automatically valuable for sparse niche apps. Play currently supports one default-graphics experiment per app or up to five localized experiments simultaneously; localized tests can change text and graphics and apply only to selected listing languages. Google recommends isolating one changed item when causal interpretation matters. Experiments can remain `More data needed`, end in a draw, recommend a variant, or keep the current listing, and auto-stop after six months.

For MintTap/LogMate, scarce eligible traffic is itself an experimental resource. Splitting it for cosmetic questions can reduce decision quality. `More data needed` remains unknown rather than a reason to manufacture traffic, weaken standards, or overinterpret directional movement. A Store install/open winner is a Store-funnel result, not proof of qualified specialist activation or repeated value.

DO0–DO5: `experiment eligibility → treatment isolation → audience/listing identity → platform-result semantics → specialist-value guardrail → rollout/replication/retirement`.

Preserve: `experiment available ≠ experiment worth running`, `traffic split ≠ free evidence`, `More data needed ≠ no effect`, `Draw ≠ proven equivalence`, `install-click winner ≠ specialist-value winner`, `localized winner ≠ global winner`, `bundle winner ≠ component causality`, `Play recommendation ≠ mandatory rollout`, `applied winner ≠ permanent truth`, and `six-month auto-stop ≠ successful experiment`.

## Progress interpretation
Zero-cost success is not page count, rankings, clicks, followers, karma, submissions, nominations, installs, campaign/UTM-token count, CSL count, experiment count, event-card count, tag count, star averages, impressions, CTR or estimated ad revenue alone. Sustainable growth requires qualified users to reach and repeat promised specialist value. Attribution improves evidence but does not convert platform credit into causal truth; sparse, aggregated or privacy-suppressed observations remain unknown rather than zero. Experiments improve evidence only when traffic, treatment identity and outcome semantics support the decision.
