## Latest validated addition — 235
Store experimentation is optional for sparse niche apps. Apple PPO and Google Play Store Listing Experiments both expose statistical controls/results, but narrow audiences can be structurally underpowered for frequent micro-tests. The operating question is whether available traffic can answer a material decision within the platform's experiment constraints—not whether an experiment tool exists.

Apple PPO supports up to three treatments in one test and a maximum 90-day run. Results appear only after at least five attributed first-time downloads; this is a reporting threshold, not sufficient evidence. Apple assigns confidence as data accumulates and can label a treatment better/worse at at least 90% confidence or potentially inconclusive when current traffic is unlikely to resolve the test. Apple's duration estimator uses existing impressions/downloads and the desired conversion improvement.

Google Play experiments currently target unique-user install clicks, open clicks, or pre-registration clicks and expose variant count, audience allocation, minimum detectable effect (MDE), and confidence level. Google may return “More data needed.” MDE is a decision-design parameter; a draw under an MDE is not proof of exact equality. More variants divide scarce traffic and can delay resolution.

EU0–EU5: `decision identity → hypothesis/treatment isolation → metric/denominator identity → power/materiality integrity → execution/stopping integrity → qualified-value decision`.

Preserve: `reporting threshold ≠ sufficient evidence`; `confidence ≠ business materiality`; `draw ≠ exact equivalence`; `inconclusive/More data needed ≠ no effect`; `Store click/download uplift ≠ retained specialist value`; `permission to run many variants ≠ reason to split scarce traffic`.
