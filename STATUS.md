## Latest validated addition — 173
Apple App Store Connect peer benchmarks are conditional comparison cohorts, not universal Store averages. Peer groups are constructed from category, business model and download-volume tier, and expose P25/P50/P75 context. Benchmark Conversion Rate has its own semantic contract: total downloads plus pre-orders divided by unique-device impressions; pre-orders are not counted again when downloaded.

Apple explicitly positions benchmark distributions as directional insight rather than exact ranking. Peer groups require sufficient size, use differential privacy/noise, and may include comparable Apple apps. Missing benchmark data therefore is not zero or evidence of underperformance.

CK0–CK5: `metric identity → peer identity → distribution identity → privacy/availability integrity → diagnostic use → product-value decision`.

Preserve `peer benchmark ≠ market size`, `peer percentile ≠ exact competitor ranking`, `below P50 ≠ proof of bad creative`, `above P75 ≠ product-market fit`, `benchmark movement ≠ causal lift`, `benchmark unavailable ≠ zero`, and `Apple benchmark Conversion Rate ≠ Google Play Store-listing CTR`.

## Progress interpretation
Zero-cost success is not clicks, followers, karma, pre-registrations, pre-orders or installs. A Store metric label is not a stable semantic contract, and a peer percentile is not an industry target or causal diagnosis. Native attribution improves observability but not incrementality. Sparse evidence remains inconclusive and suppressed/missing benchmark observations are not zero. Accessibility is a product capability first and discovery surface second. Store/platform approval or metadata is not proof of demand or product-market fit. Ad supply authorization is not revenue proof. Consent is not a one-time boolean and consent compliance does not create placement permission. A foreground event is not an eligible app-open exposure; a navigation tap is not a natural interstitial transition; an eligible transition is not unlimited exposure permission. Higher requests, impressions/user, CTR, refresh or full-screen exposure are not sustainable growth if they increase traffic-quality risk, delay first/restored useful value, interrupt professional work, manipulate privacy choice, or degrade core-task completion and repeated useful use. Sustainable growth requires qualified users to reach and repeat promised core value while advertising adds reconciled incremental revenue only from clearly identified, privacy-eligible, natural or genuinely voluntary states at exposure pressure that preserves traffic quality and product value.
