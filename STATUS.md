## Latest validated addition — 172
Store acquisition labels are not cross-platform metric contracts. Apple currently defines App Store Connect Conversion Rate as total downloads / unique impressions; total downloads include first-time downloads and redownloads. Apple App Store Search source also includes views/downloads from ads in search results, so `App Store Search` is not inherently organic-only.

Google's current Store-listing performance surface defines CTR as the percentage of listing visitors who click Install, Open or Pre-register and states that this replaces the legacy Conversion rate metric. Google monthly Store Performance exports still expose store-listing acquisitions/visitors/conversion-rate fields and traffic-source/search-term/UTM dimensions. UI labels and export schemas must therefore be versioned rather than silently normalized.

Apple CPP and individual In-App Event data require at least five first-time downloads before treatment/event-specific data is displayed. Sparse missing/suppressed observations are therefore not zero. Google organic export hierarchy also creates a double-count trap: the organic parent is the sum of organic search and browse.

CJ0–CJ5: `report/surface identity → numerator/denominator identity → acquisition-state identity → source/aggregation integrity → suppression/missingness integrity → semantically valid comparison`.

Preserve `same label ≠ same metric`, `Apple Conversion Rate ≠ Google current Store-listing CTR`, `click ≠ install`, `App Store Search ≠ organic-only`, `suppressed/missing ≠ zero`, and `organic parent + search + browse = double count`.

## Progress interpretation
Zero-cost success is not clicks, followers, karma, pre-registrations, pre-orders or installs. A Store metric label is not a stable semantic contract: numerator, denominator, source/surface, acquisition state, suppression and schema date must be preserved. Native attribution improves observability but not incrementality. Sparse evidence remains inconclusive and suppressed/missing observations are not zero. Accessibility is a product capability first and discovery surface second. Store/platform approval or metadata is not proof of demand or product-market fit. Ad supply authorization is not revenue proof. Consent is not a one-time boolean and consent compliance does not create placement permission. A foreground event is not an eligible app-open exposure; a navigation tap is not a natural interstitial transition; an eligible transition is not unlimited exposure permission. Higher requests, impressions/user, CTR, refresh or full-screen exposure are not sustainable growth if they increase traffic-quality risk, delay first/restored useful value, interrupt professional work, manipulate privacy choice, or degrade core-task completion and repeated useful use. Sustainable growth requires qualified users to reach and repeat promised core value while advertising adds reconciled incremental revenue only from clearly identified, privacy-eligible, natural or genuinely voluntary states at exposure pressure that preserves traffic quality and product value.
