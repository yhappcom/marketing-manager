## Latest validated addition — 233
Google Play accessibility discovery is not equivalent to Apple's Accessibility Nutrition Labels. Play Console currently permits accessibility tags such as Screen reader-friendly, Visual assistance, Hearing assistance, Learning disability, Motor assistance, and Accessible communication. These tags sit inside the app's maximum five-tag budget. Google says categories/tags help users discover relevant apps and accessibility tags signal compatibility with assistive technologies.

This creates a scarce-metadata decision rather than a mandate to maximize accessibility tags. Google instructs developers to select tags strongly associated with the app; their relevance should be clear to an unfamiliar user from the Store listing or initial experience. Accessibility tags must therefore follow verified product capability, not be selected merely for discovery.

Google Accessibility Scanner can flag content labels, touch-target size, clickable items, and contrast, but Google explicitly states that Scanner is not a substitute for manual testing and does not guarantee accessibility. A Scanner pass or framework semantics cannot alone authorize an accessibility marketing claim.

ES0–ES5: `platform/surface identity → capability identity → common-task evidence integrity → tag-truth integrity → tag-budget integrity → qualified-value decision`.

Preserve: `Scanner pass ≠ accessibility guarantee`; `framework semantics ≠ verified assistive-technology workflow`; `verified capability ≠ automatic tag selection`; `tag ≠ ranking guarantee`; `discovery ≠ install`; `install ≠ accessible specialist value`. Apple ER declarations and Google ES tags remain separate registries even when they draw from shared product evidence.
