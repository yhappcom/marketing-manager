## Latest validated addition — 195
Research 195 closes a newly material Apple Store-discovery governance gap. App Tags are Apple-created discovery labels derived from App Store Connect metadata using AI plus human curation; they can appear in search results, Search landing pages and product pages. Developers can review and deselect Apple-assigned tags, but should not model them as free-form developer keywords.

As of 2026-09-22, Apple states that App Tags are displayed only in the United States and default assignment is based on en_US metadata. Apple also exposes App Tag read/opt-out operations through the App Store Connect API. This makes tag state auditable and versionable, while still leaving generation/classification platform-controlled.

DG0–DG5: `territory/feature identity → metadata truth integrity → assigned-tag identity → relevance/misclassification control → discovery-route separation → qualified-growth decision`.

Preserve: `Apple-created tag ≠ developer keyword`, `tag presence ≠ search rank guarantee`, `tag relevance ≠ query-level attribution`, `more tags ≠ better qualified discovery`, `US tag behavior ≠ global Store behavior`, `metadata written to provoke AI classification ≠ trustworthy ASO`, and `CPP keyword routing ≠ App Tag routing`.

## Progress interpretation
Zero-cost success is not page count, rankings, clicks, followers, karma, submissions, installs, tag count, star averages, review counts, impressions, CTR, rewarded acceptance or estimated ad revenue alone. Sustainable growth requires qualified users to reach and repeat the promised specialist value. Store discovery metadata and Apple-created tags must remain truthful representations of supported specialist jobs, not mechanisms for manufacturing broad relevance. Advertising contributes only incremental, reconciled revenue from privacy-eligible, non-intrusive states through an authorized and active supply path. Product release readiness, Store-discovery integrity, feedback integrity, ad-supply activation readiness and monetization-experiment readiness are separate tracks.
