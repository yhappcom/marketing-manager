## Latest validated addition — 223
Google AdMob frequency caps can constrain impressions per user at app and ad-unit level over minute/hour/day windows. App-level caps cover interstitial, rewarded and app-open units; when app-level and ad-unit-level caps coexist, the first reached governs serving. Google states caps apply to Google demand and third-party ad sources. Changes can take up to 24 hours to take effect, and slight server delay can occasionally exceed the configured cap.

The operational correction is that a frequency cap is a delivery ceiling, not a desired impression quota and not proof of acceptable UX. Specialist apps therefore use an exposure budget: cap configuration plus format, placement, workflow state, session density, user-level exposure distribution and interruption cost.

EI0–EI5: `configuration identity → effective-cap integrity → delivery-versus-target integrity → exposure-budget integrity → change-window integrity → sustainable-value decision`.

Preserve: `frequency cap ≠ frequency target`; `cap reached ≠ healthy monetization`; `below cap ≠ non-intrusive UX`; `app-level cap ≠ ad-unit-level cap`; `configured cap ≠ instantly effective cap`; `configured cap ≠ perfectly hard real-time ceiling`; `average density ≠ individual exposure safety`; and `revenue/user increase ≠ sustainable improvement when exposure burden rises materially`.

## Progress interpretation
Zero-cost success is not rankings, clicks, followers, subscribers, installs, Store conversion, generic retention, peer percentile, impressions, CTR, dashboard eCPM, ARPV or estimated impression revenue alone. Sustainable growth requires qualified users to reach and repeat promised specialist value through a reliable experience, with monetization that does not corrupt that value. Attribution is not causality; sparse/aggregated/private/unavailable or threshold-suppressed observations remain unknown rather than zero. Advertising decisions retain exact format, placement, geometry, lifecycle, traffic quality, delivered-content/control state, auction/floor configuration, exposure distribution, frequency-cap/exposure-budget state and revenue semantics. The product does not manufacture friction, accidental interaction, inappropriate creative exposure, concentrate excessive ad burden on frequent specialist users, or sacrifice specialist readability merely to create additional ad inventory, approach a cap, or improve eCPM/ARPV.
