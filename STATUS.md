## Latest validated addition — 111
Canonical principle: **Privacy disclosure is product evidence first and acquisition trust evidence second. Never optimize the label; optimize the underlying data practice and make every public disclosure match it.**

Apple explicitly exposes Privacy Nutrition Labels on App Store product pages before download and requires privacy-practice information for new apps/updates. Developers are responsible for keeping answers accurate/current and for including relevant third-party partner behavior. Privacy manifests provide a supply-chain bridge: Xcode combines manifests from app/third-party code into a report that can support accurate Store disclosure. Apple also maintains privacy-manifest/signature requirements for listed commonly used SDKs.

No claim was established that a particular privacy label mechanically improves Store ranking or conversion. Privacy is therefore governed as trust/integrity evidence, not an ASO keyword tactic.

AC0–AC5 governs privacy-disclosure parity. AC0 covers known contradictions or materially false privacy claims. AC3 is the minimum deliberate acquisition state: shipping-version data-flow inventory, first/third-party flow ownership, Store/privacy-policy parity, applicable tracking/consent review, SDK-change triggers, and marketing claims constrained to verified behavior. AC4 requires parity to survive releases/dependency updates; AC5 is reusable across apps.

For MintTap, ad/analytics/Firebase/crash-reporting/network behavior must be audited before using claims such as private, anonymous, local, no tracking, or no financial-data collection. U/E monetization experiments re-enter AC review whenever an SDK/configuration can materially change data handling.

For LogMate, offline-first/no-login must not be translated into `no data leaves the device` unless the complete production stack supports that stronger claim. Ads, analytics, crash reporting, update checks, PWA/sync and backup paths require independent classification.
