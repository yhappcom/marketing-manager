## Latest validated addition — 211
Google Play exposes retention-adjacent metrics with materially different semantics. A retained installer is an installer who kept the app installed for the specified period; Google explicitly notes this does not mean the app was opened. Behavioral user-retention metrics in Statistics are based on qualifying app-open behavior and use different cohort definitions. Neither establishes repeated specialist value.

DW0–DW5: `metric identity → cohort-anchor integrity → event integrity → sparse/aggregation integrity → diagnostic triangulation → growth/monetization decision`.

Preserve: `kept installed ≠ reopened ≠ repeated specialist value ≠ monetizable repeat session ≠ sustainable revenue`. Every retention observation must preserve platform, surface, exact metric, cohort anchor, denominator, event, window, geography, source/filter state and sparsity state. Never merge Google retained-installer persistence, Google behavioral retention, Apple retention and internal repeat-value events into a synthetic company retention percentage.

Operational consequence: diagnose the progression from Store acquisition to install persistence to actual app use to repeated specialist workflow. Scale zero-cost routes only when users progress toward repeated specialist value. A source producing many retained installers but little repeated specialist utility is not a validated growth winner. Ad intrusion must not be increased merely to compensate for weak downstream value.

## Progress interpretation
Zero-cost success is not rankings, clicks, followers, subscribers, installs, Store conversion, install persistence, generic retention, peer percentile, experiment count, impressions, CTR or estimated ad revenue alone. Sustainable growth requires qualified users to reach and repeat promised specialist value. Apple and Google Play benchmarks provide contextual diagnosis, not competitive truth or targets, and their percentile systems must not be merged. Retention-like metrics must retain their exact event and cohort semantics. Attribution improves evidence but does not convert platform credit into causal truth; sparse, aggregated, private or unavailable observations remain unknown rather than zero.
