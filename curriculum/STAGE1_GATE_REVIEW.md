## Stage 1 decision

## Genuine remaining knowledge gaps

### Gap 1 — sparse-sample experimentation

This is the highest-priority theoretical gap. MintTap/LogMate serve narrow populations, so conventional high-volume A/B-test habits can produce unstable winners, underpowered experiments, repeated-peeking errors and oversegmentation. Stage 2 needs an operational framework covering effect size, uncertainty, sequential decisions, practical vs statistical significance, minimum sample/evidence rules and `INCONCLUSIVE` outcomes without pretending that a universal sample-size threshold exists.

### Gap 2 — portfolio resource allocation

As yhappcom adds apps, 'zero-cost' channels compete for the same owner/marketing/design/engineering time. The company needs a portfolio-level method for deciding which app/channel/content experiment receives scarce labor. Existing fully-loaded-cost work is necessary but not sufficient. This becomes important before several apps require simultaneous growth work.

## Not knowledge gaps — evidence gaps

Do not respond to these by writing more generic research:
- MintTap/LogMate Store performance;
- actual search terms;
- activation definitions;
- retention rates/cadence;
- ad revenue and placement effects;
- community permission states;
- social ROI;
- launch cohort quality.

These require current product/platform/user evidence.

## Stage 2 entry sequence

1. Build sparse-niche experimentation and decision-quality foundation.
2. Then move to product evidence whenever accessible rather than continuing abstract research.
3. Build portfolio labor-allocation system before multiple simultaneous app growth programs make the issue operationally material.
4. Convert real MintTap/LogMate work into case studies that revise the playbook.

## Gate conclusion

Stage 1 passes as a broad marketing operating foundation. The next useful learning is not another channel overview; it is deeper decision science for small specialist samples.
