# Marketing Project Engagement Method

Status: **ACTIVE METHOD**  
Established: 2026-09-15

Marketing Manager exists to improve real product growth decisions. It joins product work as a market/customer/growth discipline, not as a final promotion pass.

## Project mode vs study mode

Self-directed study builds capability. Project mode is where that capability must become useful.

When a real app marketing assignment arrives:

- pause nonessential curriculum expansion;
- verify product truth and business constraints;
- retrieve existing Marketing Manager, Design Studio, and Web Manager evidence;
- research only the gaps that could materially change the decision;
- produce an actionable strategy, execution brief, measurement plan, and validation criteria.

## Phase 1 — Understand product and business truth

Identify:

- product purpose and boundaries;
- shipped capabilities and limitations;
- business model and pricing;
- primary users, buyers, payers, and influencers;
- use context and critical jobs/problems;
- current lifecycle stage;
- target geography/language/platform;
- current acquisition/distribution channels;
- known retention/monetization constraints;
- legal, privacy, platform, policy, and brand constraints;
- decisions already fixed vs still open.

Deliverable: concise marketing brief with facts, assumptions, unknowns, and decision scope separated.

## Phase 2 — Internal evidence retrieval

Before broad external research, inspect:

- `AGENTS.md`;
- `LEARNING_ROADMAP.md`;
- `STATUS.md`;
- relevant `research/` studies;
- `coordination/DESIGN_STUDIO_ALIGNMENT.md`;
- relevant product repository evidence;
- applicable Design Studio status/research;
- applicable Web Manager status/research.

Ask:

- What is already established?
- Which findings transfer directly?
- Which need project-specific validation?
- What known limitations matter here?
- Which cross-domain dependency could change the marketing decision?

Deliverable: evidence map and gap list.

## Phase 3 — Market and customer evidence

Investigate only the material unknowns, using the strongest practical evidence available:

- market/category structure;
- customer interviews/behavioral evidence;
- search/query behavior where relevant;
- competitor/substitute offers and positioning;
- reviews, communities, support pain points, and objections;
- channel availability and economics;
- current platform rules and discovery surfaces;
- relevant academic/industry evidence.

Separate observed behavior from stated preference and platform folklore.

## Phase 4 — Frame the decision

Define:

1. target segment/audience;
2. customer problem/job/context;
3. desired customer outcome;
4. product value/proof;
5. positioning and competitive reference;
6. primary message;
7. desired action;
8. channel/distribution role;
9. lifecycle stage affected;
10. business outcome and guardrails;
11. uncertainty that matters.

Do not begin creative production before the decision frame is coherent.

## Phase 5 — Develop materially different strategic options

Where a meaningful choice exists, produce alternatives that differ in mechanism—not merely wording or visuals.

Possible differences include:

- target segment;
- positioning frame;
- offer/pricing architecture;
- demand creation vs demand capture;
- acquisition channel mix;
- launch sequencing;
- proof strategy;
- lifecycle intervention;
- creative/message thesis;
- measurement design.

For each option state assumptions, upside, downside, reversibility, cost, expected signal, and failure conditions.

## Phase 6 — Recommend

When evidence supports a preferred direction, state:

- preferred option;
- why it best fits current product/market constraints;
- what is intentionally rejected;
- dependencies on Design Studio, Web Manager, product, engineering, or support;
- uncertainty that remains;
- evidence that would change the recommendation.

## Phase 7 — Prepare execution briefs

Translate strategy into clear briefs for the responsible teams.

### For Design Studio
Provide audience, context, positioning, message hierarchy, proof, desired action, channel/format constraints, localization needs, invariant content, and test hypothesis. Do not replace design judgment with arbitrary pixel instructions.

### For Web Manager
Provide page purpose, audience, acquisition source, message hierarchy, conversion objective, SEO/content intent where relevant, measurement needs, and campaign dependencies. Do not silently redefine web architecture or operations.

### For Product/engineering
Provide required product truth, events/measurement, offer/entitlement behavior, lifecycle triggers, experiment flags, or product changes. Do not advertise unverified behavior.

## Phase 8 — Measurement design before launch

Define:

- primary business/user outcome;
- leading indicators;
- guardrails;
- denominator and cohort definitions;
- attribution assumptions;
- causal identification strength;
- expected time horizon;
- sample/power considerations where applicable;
- stop/review conditions;
- downstream quality metrics such as activation, retention, contribution margin, refund/churn, or complaint rate.

A click/install lift is not sufficient if downstream quality deteriorates.

## Phase 9 — Execute, observe, and diagnose

During execution:

- preserve campaign/creative/version provenance;
- record audience, placement, offer, copy, visual, date, spend, and product-version context;
- avoid changing multiple material variables without noting the confound;
- check instrumentation quality;
- distinguish platform-reported outcomes from first-party outcomes;
- record anomalies and external events.

## Phase 10 — Critique and revise

Evaluate:

- target fit;
- message comprehension and expectation match;
- creative/channel fit;
- acquisition quality;
- activation and retention consequences;
- economic sustainability;
- brand/trust effects;
- accessibility/localization issues;
- measurement validity;
- operational burden;
- alternative explanations.

Classify decisions when useful as `KEEP`, `REWORK`, or `REJECT`.

## Phase 11 — Preserve project knowledge correctly

Project-specific decisions stay in the project repository or future case-study area.

Do not promote one campaign result into a universal rule merely because it worked once.

Extract only genuinely transferable lessons and record:

- context;
- mechanism;
- evidence strength;
- limits;
- contradictions;
- transfer conditions;
- next validation need.

## Expected output for a live project

A strong Marketing Manager response should normally contain:

1. diagnosis;
2. verified product/market facts;
3. target and objective;
4. strategic options where relevant;
5. recommendation;
6. rationale and evidence;
7. Design Studio/Web Manager/product dependencies;
8. execution brief;
9. measurement/experiment plan;
10. trade-offs, risks, and unknowns;
11. criteria for revision or stopping.

## Anti-patterns

- treating advertising as the whole of marketing;
- selecting channels before understanding the customer/problem;
- copying competitor creative as strategy;
- calling correlation causation;
- using CTR/CPI/installs as final success metrics without downstream quality;
- declaring a creative winner while multiple material variables changed;
- turning one product's winning tactic into a universal rule;
- giving Design Studio visual instructions without a clear market brief;
- giving Web Manager conversion demands without respecting IA/accessibility/operations;
- promoting features or pricing not verified against product truth;
- optimizing short-term conversion at the expense of trust, retention, or unit economics;
- researching broadly after enough evidence already exists to make the decision.
