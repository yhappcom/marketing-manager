# yhappcom Marketing Playbook v1

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: STAGE 1 SYNTHESIS — operational baseline, not a claim of live validation

## Purpose

This playbook converts Foundation research 001–019 into one reusable operating system for cash-constrained specialist apps. It is the default marketing decision sequence for MintTap, LogMate and future niche apps unless product evidence requires a documented exception.

The system optimizes for qualified retained usage and sustainable advertising revenue, not raw reach, followers or installs.

## 1. Governing objective and constraints

Primary operating objective:

`relevant demand → credible promise → qualified acquisition → meaningful activation → repeated core value → sustainable ad-bearing use`

Hard constraints:
- default to little/no direct cash acquisition spend;
- labor and opportunity cost are real costs;
- no intrusive, deceptive or access-limiting advertising;
- do not manufacture attribution certainty where privacy/platform limits prevent it;
- specialist trust and factual accuracy outrank short-term click/conversion gains;
- community access is permission-dependent, not an entitlement;
- platform-native metric definitions are preserved rather than falsely normalized.

## 2. Company marketing sequence

### Gate A — Market/problem evidence

Before distribution work, identify:
- narrow target population;
- problem/situation/category-entry-point evidence;
- active vs problem-aware vs latent demand;
- switching/search/trust barriers;
- actual language used by target users.

Evidence priority: observed product/support/search/community evidence > authoritative category evidence > reasoned hypothesis.

Do not solve weak problem evidence with more channels.

### Gate B — Positioning and proof

Define:
- category frame;
- priority user/situation;
- relevant promise;
- product proof supporting that promise;
- trust/risk-reduction requirements.

Visual identity is not positioning. Repeated brand elements are not called distinctive assets until buyer recognition/uniqueness is evidenced.

### Gate C — Store readiness

For each store:
1. map verified intent;
2. establish default listing proposition;
3. create intent-specific pages only when distinct demand warrants them;
4. make screenshots/creative product proof, not decoration;
5. use native experiments under native metric definitions;
6. validate downstream activation/retention before declaring a marketing winner.

Never combine Apple and Google metrics into a synthetic conversion metric that erases their definitions.

### Gate D — Measurement readiness

Minimum diagnostic chain:

`source → store/landing → install/download signal → first open → activation → retained core-value use → eligible ad opportunity → request/match/show → revenue`

Every KPI has numerator, denominator, population, window and source. Data states: OBSERVED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN; privacy-suppressed sparse cohorts remain INCONCLUSIVE rather than zero.

### Gate E — Owned content

Create content only when it performs a durable job:
- capture verified active demand;
- answer recurring specialist questions;
- develop problem-aware demand;
- provide evidence/proof that cannot fit in a Store page;
- reduce switching/trust uncertainty.

Content loop:
`real problem/CEP → useful original asset → discoverability → problem resolution → qualified product/store handoff → activation evidence → refresh/retire decision`

Avoid thin template factories and content-volume targets. High-risk finance/regulatory material requires primary-source verification and maintenance discipline.

### Gate F — Community

Community operating sequence:
`audience relevance → current local permission → native standalone value → disclosure where relevant → learning → permitted product handoff → feedback into product/store/content`

Use permission states: ALLOWED_EXPLICIT / ALLOWED_CONDITIONAL / AMBIGUOUS / PROHIBITED / UNKNOWN-STALE.

A contribution should remain useful if the product link is removed. Deletion/warning triggers stop-and-review, not evasion or reposting.

### Gate G — Selective social

A social channel is admitted only if there is evidence for:
- target audience presence;
- context fit;
- repeatable content job;
- proof-carrying ability;
- measurable learning/acquisition value;
- acceptable labor cost.

Otherwise PARK it. Do not operate every major platform by default. Platform-native audience/view metrics are not interchangeable.

### Gate H — Launch sequencing

Launch is a staged evidence process, not one announcement day:
`readiness → seed owned assets → permission-checked community → optional prelaunch intent capture → live path verification → controlled channel activation → first-cohort activation/retention diagnosis → expansion → evergreen loops`

Pre-order/pre-registration is optional. Launch-day downloads are not a success criterion by themselves.

### Gate I — Activation and retention

Activation means first meaningful product value, not install, first_open, registration or onboarding completion unless evidence proves equivalence.

Separate app-open retention from core-value/workflow retention. Specialist utilities should be judged on natural workflow cadence, not arbitrary daily-use norms.

If acquisition rises while activation/retention falls, investigate promise/audience mismatch before adding traffic.

### Gate J — Advertising monetization

Advertising is downstream of retained value:
`retained use → legitimate task-state opportunity → compliant/timely ad → acceptable exposure pressure → realized revenue`

Do not infer that every screen/session is an ad opportunity. Protect precision, data-entry, conflict-resolution, backup/restore and other trust-critical states from disruptive full-screen ads.

Experiment unit:
`format × task-state placement × eligibility × exposure cap × interval/session rule × app version`

Evaluate revenue with task completion, abandonment, retention, latency, complaints and policy signals. A missed timing window is generally a skipped impression, not permission to interrupt later.

## 3. Channel allocation rule

Rank zero/low-cash channels using:

`Audience Fit × Permission Gate × Demand-State Fit × Proof Fit × Durability × Measurement/Learning Value × Effort Efficiency`

A free channel with weak audience fit and high labor cost is not cheap.

## 4. Diagnostic order when growth is weak

Do not default to more promotion. Diagnose in order:
1. discoverability;
2. intent/audience quality;
3. Store/message/proof conversion;
4. first-use/activation;
5. core-value retention;
6. durable reacquisition/distribution;
7. monetization execution.

Repair the earliest material bottleneck supported by evidence.

## 5. Promise-consistency contract

The same defensible promise must survive:
`community/social/content → Store metadata → screenshot proof → first-use experience → ongoing product reality → monetization`

A channel can increase conversion while reducing business value if it attracts the wrong audience or overstates capability, effort, scope or monetization.

## 6. MintTap current application boundary

Current evidence-bounded hypotheses:
- specialist audience: YieldMax-oriented investors;
- candidate situations: distribution/ROC analysis, reverse-split continuity, portfolio/cost-basis/reinvestment complexity;
- owned educational/reference content and specialist community are plausible high-fit surfaces;
- finance-related factual content requires elevated sourcing/trust controls;
- activation, retention cadence, search demand, current Store performance and ad implementation remain live-validation items.

Do not convert these hypotheses into performance claims without telemetry/search/user evidence.

## 7. LogMate current application boundary

Current evidence-bounded hypotheses:
- specialist audience: airline/professional pilots, with segment/geography still open;
- candidate situations: record migration/import, routine flight logging, backup/export, historical record retrieval and data ownership;
- specialist workflow education and pilot communities may be high-fit surfaces subject to permission and geography;
- jurisdiction-specific logbook/regulatory claims require current primary authority verification;
- launch segment, activation, natural retention cadence, demand and ad model remain live-validation items.

## 8. Reusable launch/growth record for future apps

Every future app should maintain one concise record containing:
- target population and exclusion boundary;
- verified problem/CEP evidence;
- demand-state map;
- positioning/proof contract;
- Store intent/listing map;
- source/campaign registry;
- activation hypothesis and validation status;
- natural retention cadence hypothesis;
- owned-content inventory;
- community permission ledger;
- social channel states: TEST / ACTIVE / PARKED / STOPPED;
- launch readiness and channel sequence;
- ad task-state map and experiment registry;
- major UNKNOWNs and next evidence needed.

## 9. Evidence discipline

Use labels consistently:
- FOUNDATION COMPLETE — principle/framework is sufficiently established for use;
- APPLIED-HYPOTHESIS — framework has been mapped to a product but lacks live confirmation;
- LIVE VALIDATION REQUIRED — decision requires current product/user/platform data;
- NOT YET BASELINED — capability lacks sufficient foundation and needs further study.

Never promote APPLIED-HYPOTHESIS to fact because it appears repeatedly in internal documents.

## 10. What Stage 1 does not prove

Stage 1 establishes a decision system. It does not prove:
- MintTap or LogMate channel-market fit;
- Store conversion benchmarks;
- activation definitions;
- retention cadence or rates;
- community permissions not explicitly checked;
- social channel ROI;
- ad placement profitability or retention impact;
- launch performance.

Those are Stage 2/live-evidence problems.

## 11. Stage 2 operating direction

Stage 2 should deepen only capabilities that need expert specialization or live application. Priority order:
1. experimentation/statistical decision quality for sparse niche samples;
2. product-specific Store/search/community evidence collection;
3. activation/retention validation methods;
4. ad monetization economics with real telemetry;
5. content/search portfolio management with Search Console evidence;
6. launch and channel case studies from actual products.

This playbook is revised when evidence changes the operating rules; it is not expanded merely to accumulate documents.
