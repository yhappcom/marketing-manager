## 1. Working definition

`SOURCE` — The American Marketing Association currently defines marketing as the activity, institutions, and processes involved in creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large.

Source: American Marketing Association, “What is Marketing? — The Definition of Marketing,” reviewed 2026-09-15.  
https://www.ama.org/the-definition-of-marketing-what-is-marketing/

### Synthesis

Marketing cannot be reduced to advertising because the definition explicitly spans at least four linked activities:

- **creating** an offering/value proposition;
- **communicating** why it matters;
- **delivering** it through an accessible channel/system;
- **exchanging** value under terms acceptable to the parties involved.

Promotion therefore operates inside a larger market system. A strong advertisement cannot permanently repair a weak product, unacceptable price, inaccessible distribution, poor onboarding, low trust, or lack of customer value.

`MARKETING JUDGMENT` — For an app company, product experience, store availability, pricing/subscription architecture, trial design, App Store/Google Play listing, website, support, reputation, onboarding, and advertising can all affect marketing outcomes. The organizational owner may differ, but the customer experiences one combined value system.

---

## 2. The basic unit is exchange, not media exposure

The recurring core in modern definitions is **exchange around value**. Media exposure, clicks, impressions, and installs are intermediate events; none proves that a durable value exchange occurred.

A useful foundation model is:

`customer problem/context → offered value → discovery/understanding → access → exchange → use experience → realized value → repeat/retention/referral/business outcome`

This is deliberately broader than a linear advertising funnel.

### Why this matters

If an app receives many impressions but few installs, the failure could involve message relevance, audience, store conversion, trust, perceived value, competition, or friction. If installs are high but retention collapses, increasing ad spend may amplify acquisition while worsening economics. Marketing diagnosis therefore requires tracing the whole exchange/value system rather than assigning every weak outcome to “promotion.”

## 3. Marketing thought expanded beyond a goods-and-transaction view

`SOURCE` — Vargo and Lusch’s 2004 *Journal of Marketing* paper described a historical shift away from a dominant logic centered on tangible goods, embedded value, and transactions toward perspectives emphasizing intangible resources, relationships, service provision, and value co-creation.

Source: Stephen L. Vargo & Robert F. Lusch, “Evolving to a New Dominant Logic for Marketing,” *Journal of Marketing*, 68(1), 2004.  
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.68.1.1.24036

### Important limitation

`SYNTHESIS` — Service-dominant logic is an influential theoretical perspective, not a license to declare that all value questions are settled. It is useful because it forces the manager to separate an offering from the value ultimately realized in use. Later research should compare it with competing/adjacent marketing and economic frameworks rather than treating one school as doctrine.

### App implication

For software, the distinction is especially useful. The downloaded binary or subscription entitlement is not identical to the user’s realized outcome. A logbook app, finance tracker, or utility creates business value only when users can repeatedly accomplish the job they hired it to do. Acquisition claims must therefore remain anchored to actual product use.

## 4. Customer orientation and market orientation are related but not identical

`SOURCE` — Kohli and Jaworski’s 1990 *Journal of Marketing* work formalized market orientation as an organization-level construct and focused on how the marketing concept is implemented, drawing on literature and field interviews across functions and organizations.

Source: Ajay K. Kohli & Bernard J. Jaworski, “Market Orientation: The Construct, Research Propositions, and Managerial Implications,” *Journal of Marketing*, 54(2), 1990.  
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224299005400201

`SOURCE` — A 2022 literature review characterizes customer orientation as rooted in the broader market-orientation literature and documents decades of research across marketing and related fields.

Source: Xin Zhao, “Customer Orientation: A Literature Review Based on Bibliometric Analysis,” 2022.  
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440221079804

### Synthesis

Being “customer-focused” should not mean mechanically implementing every stated customer request. Professional marketing must synthesize customer needs with competitor/substitute behavior, product capability, economics, distribution, regulation, and long-term strategy.

`MARKETING JUDGMENT` — User feedback is evidence, not an automatic product roadmap. The manager must understand what underlying problem the feedback reveals, how broadly it occurs, what alternatives users have, and whether solving it creates sustainable value.

## 5. Where the marketing mix came from

The familiar 4P framework is historically downstream of a broader “marketing mix” idea.

`SOURCE` — A Harvard Business School historical review describes Neil Borden’s 1964 “The Concept of the Marketing Mix” as cataloging a broad set of interrelated managerial decisions. Borden’s reasoning explicitly treated advertising as only one element of a total marketing program.

Source: John A. Quelch & Katherine E. Jocz, historical review discussing Borden’s marketing-mix contribution, Harvard Business School, 2008.  
https://www.hbs.edu/research/RePEc/hbs/journl/2008Q4quelch.pdf

`SOURCE` — Later literature records that E. Jerome McCarthy simplified the larger marketing-mix concept into the four widely taught categories: **Product, Price, Place, Promotion**.

Source: “Revising the structural framework for marketing management,” *Journal of Business Research* / ScienceDirect record.  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296302005027

### What the 4Ps are good for

They are a compact checklist reminding a manager that marketing decisions extend beyond promotion:

- **Product** — what is offered and what problem/value it addresses;
- **Price** — the economic terms of exchange;
- **Place** — how the offering becomes available/reachable;
- **Promotion** — how awareness, understanding, preference, trial, or action are influenced.

### What the 4Ps are not

They are not a complete strategy, causal model, customer journey, research method, or measurement framework.

`SOURCE` — Van Waterschoot and Van den Bulte’s 1992 *Journal of Marketing* critique notes that the 4P classification had wide acceptance but also classification problems, including lack of mutual exclusiveness and collective exhaustiveness.

Source: Walter van Waterschoot & Christophe Van den Bulte, “The 4P Classification of the Marketing Mix Revisited,” *Journal of Marketing*, 56(4), 1992.  
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224299205600407

`SOURCE` — Services-marketing literature also challenged the sufficiency of a four-variable mix for service contexts.

Source: “When marketing services, 4 Ps are not enough,” *Business Horizons*, 29(3), 1986.  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0007681386900078

### Marketing judgment

Use 4Ps as a **managerial coverage check**, not as an ideology. In app marketing, “place” includes digital distribution and discovery systems; “product” and “promotion” interact strongly because screenshots, onboarding, trials, reviews, and product demonstrations can simultaneously communicate and expose actual product experience.

## 6. Distinguishing adjacent disciplines

The following distinctions are working boundaries, not claims that organizations must use these exact job titles.

### Marketing
The broad market/value system: understanding markets and customers; shaping offers, positioning, pricing, distribution, communication, relationships, measurement, and sustainable demand/business outcomes.

### Advertising
Paid placement of messages/media intended to influence a defined audience. Advertising is one possible marketing instrument.

### Promotion
A broader class of communication/incentive activity that can include advertising, sales promotion, PR, creator activity, launches, and other demand-influencing mechanisms depending on the taxonomy used.

### Sales
Direct processes that convert prospective demand into transactions/contracts, especially prominent in higher-consideration or B2B contexts. Sales and marketing can share customer intelligence but are not interchangeable.

### Product marketing
The interface between product truth and market understanding: segmentation/targeting context, positioning, messaging, launch, competitive intelligence, enablement, adoption, and market feedback. Exact organizational scope varies.

### Growth
A cross-functional operating approach focused on measurable expansion of acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, and/or referral through product, marketing, data, and experimentation. “Growth” is not automatically a replacement for marketing and should not be equated with short-term hacks.

`OPEN` — Organizational definitions of product marketing and growth vary significantly. Later stages should compare professional bodies, major operating models, and real team structures before adopting precise internal role boundaries.

## 7. The first app-marketing mental model

For later yhappcom projects, begin with this system rather than “How do we advertise the app?”

### A. Market reality
- What problem/context exists?
- Who experiences it?
- What alternatives/substitutes already satisfy it?
- How important/frequent is the problem?

### B. Offer/value
- What outcome can the product credibly enable?
- What evidence supports that claim?
- What costs, risks, learning, or switching barriers does the customer perceive?

### C. Access/distribution
- Where can the target customer discover, evaluate, obtain, and use the product?
- What platform/channel constraints shape availability?

### D. Communication
- What must the customer understand or believe to evaluate the offer accurately?
- Which claims can be proven?

### E. Exchange/economics
- What does the user give: money, time, attention, data, effort, switching cost?
- What does the company receive: revenue, usage, data under consent, referral, strategic option value?

### F. Realized outcome
- Does the product actually create the promised user value after acquisition?
- Do activation, retention, and willingness to recommend support the acquisition story?

### G. Measurement and learning
- Which observable events are proxies?
- Which business/user outcomes matter?
- What evidence would distinguish correlation from causal impact?

## 8. Common foundation errors to avoid

1. **Marketing = advertising.** False category reduction.
2. **More installs = better marketing.** Installs can be low-quality or economically destructive.
3. **The customer asked for it, therefore build it.** Stated preference is evidence, not proof of durable demand.
4. **4Ps = complete marketing strategy.** They are a useful classification/checklist with known limitations.
5. **A funnel describes causality.** A funnel is a representation; customer behavior can loop, skip, reverse, delay, and occur across channels.
6. **A platform metric is the business outcome.** Clicks/attributed conversions can be proxies and may not represent incremental or profitable value.
7. **Promotion can compensate indefinitely for weak realized value.** Acquisition can temporarily mask product-value problems but usually cannot create durable retention by itself.
8. **One modern theory replaces all earlier theory.** Marketing contains competing and complementary schools; models must be selected for the decision problem.

## 9. Foundation propositions retained for future work

`SYNTHESIS`

- Marketing is a system for understanding, creating, communicating, enabling, and sustaining value exchange; promotion is only one subsystem.
- The product and its usage experience are part of marketing reality even when another team owns implementation.
- Customer orientation must be combined with broader market intelligence and business constraints.
- Frameworks such as 4Ps are maps, not reality; usefulness depends on the decision being made.
- App growth must eventually reconcile acquisition with realized user value, retention, and unit economics.
- Measurement must be designed around decisions and causal mechanisms, not around whichever platform metrics are easiest to obtain.

Before moving to ASO or channel tactics, Stage 1 should deepen:

- needs, wants, demand, utility, willingness to pay, and opportunity cost;
- consumer choice under uncertainty and bounded rationality;
