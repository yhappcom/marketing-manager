# 008 — Product-specific Zero / Low-cash Acquisition Channel Matrix

Last verified: 2026-09-15

## Purpose

Convert the earlier mechanism-level organic acquisition framework into a product-specific decision system for MintTap and LogMate. This is not a list of places where an app could be mentioned. It is a prioritization model for scarce owner/marketing time under yhappcom's low-cash constraint.

## Core rule

A channel is useful only when it can repeatedly produce relevant retained users at acceptable fully loaded effort and without violating platform/community permission or product trust.

`Channel value ≠ reach`

Working model:

`Expected channel value = eligible audience × intent/relevance × permission × message/proof fit × conversion × activation × retention × learning/durability ÷ fully-loaded effort`

Permission is a hard gate for promotional tactics. If direct promotion is prohibited or unverified, that tactic receives no acquisition score. The same surface can still retain value as a research or contribution channel.

## Scoring dimensions

Use 0–5 unless marked as a gate:

- Audience fit (AF): concentration of genuinely relevant prospective users.
- Intent proximity (IP): how close the audience's current task/problem is to app adoption.
- Proof fit (PF): whether the channel can credibly demonstrate the product's relevant capability.
- Durability (DU): whether work remains discoverable/useful after publication.
- Learning value (LV): quality of product/message/problem feedback obtainable.
- Compounding potential (CP): whether accumulated assets, reputation, links, subscribers, or indexed content improve later acquisition.
- Measurement feasibility (MF): ability to distinguish source → store/landing page → activation.
- Effort efficiency (EE): expected output relative to owner/editorial/moderation/design effort. High score = more efficient.
- Permission (PG): PASS / RESTRICTED / UNVERIFIED / FAIL for the specific tactic.

Do not add the scores into a fake-precision universal number before evidence exists. Use them to expose trade-offs and select tests.

## Cross-product conclusion

The strongest low-cash portfolio is not 'post everywhere.' It is a layered system:

1. **Store conversion infrastructure** — captures high-intent demand and converts traffic from every other source.
2. **Owned durable problem-solving content** — compounds qualified discovery and provides linkable proof.
3. **Specialist communities as listening/contribution systems** — high learning value; promotion only where permission is explicit.
4. **Owned community/support surfaces** — trust, feedback, retention and referral infrastructure.
5. **Selective social experiments** — only after audience concentration and content-format fit are demonstrated.

This creates a demand-capture bias appropriate to an early, cash-constrained niche-app company. Demand creation still matters, but broad awareness should not consume scarce labor until product-specific evidence shows it can reach relevant users efficiently.

## Test order

For both products:

1. define source-tagging and activation before scaling channels;
2. establish store-page baseline by source/territory/device where available;
3. create a small number of durable high-intent owned content assets;
4. use specialist communities to identify language, uncertainty, objections, and workflows;
5. seek explicit permission before any external-community commercial participation;
6. test one social format/channel at a time only with a measurable hypothesis;
7. compare channels on activated/retained users and fully-loaded effort, not clicks or followers alone.

## Stop / reduce conditions

Reduce or stop a tactic when repeated tests show one or more of:

- permission risk or moderator friction;
- irrelevant traffic despite apparent reach;
- installs without activation/retention;
- content labor exceeds durable qualified discovery/learning value;
- social engagement does not translate into relevant site/store behavior;
- community participation becomes product-first and damages trust;
- measurement is too weak to distinguish genuine acquisition from vanity activity.

## Evidence boundaries

This is an evidence-bounded first-pass matrix, not a claim that every Tier A channel is already producing users. Current product analytics, store-query data, search-demand data, and channel attribution remain missing. Scores are therefore hypotheses to prioritize measurement, not ROI estimates.
