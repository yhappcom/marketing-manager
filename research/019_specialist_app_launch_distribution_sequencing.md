# 019 — Specialist-App Launch & Distribution Sequencing System

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: FOUNDATION / REUSABLE OPERATING SYSTEM

## Purpose

A launch is not a single announcement day. For a cash-constrained specialist app, launch is a controlled sequence that converts pre-existing audience/problem evidence into store discovery, qualified activation, retention learning and sustainable distribution without creating an artificial one-day traffic spike.

This block integrates research 006–018 into a reusable release system for MintTap, LogMate and future niche apps.

## 1. Core model

`evidence readiness → product/release readiness → store readiness → measurement readiness → seed owned assets → permission-checked community seeding → optional pre-order/pre-registration → controlled release → qualified activation observation → retention/promise diagnosis → distribution expansion → repeatable evergreen acquisition`

The objective is not maximum launch-day downloads. It is to create the first interpretable cohorts and establish distribution loops that remain useful after launch week.

## 2. Launch readiness gates

Do not choose a public launch date merely because the binary is technically ready.

### Gate A — Audience/problem evidence
- named specialist audience and launch geography/segment;
- priority CEP/problem states;
- evidence source for each priority problem;
- explicit positioning/proposition hypothesis;
- known switching/trust barriers.

### Gate B — Product/value readiness
- core promised workflow actually works;
- activation hypothesis can be instrumented or observed;
- backup/data/trust-critical flows appropriate to the product are credible;
- known defects do not contradict the launch promise.

### Gate C — Store readiness
- default listing expresses category, user/problem and proof;
- screenshots/creative demonstrate real product behavior rather than unsupported claims;
- localization/territory decisions are intentional;
- intent-specific CPP/CSL only where distinct demand evidence exists;
- native platform metric definitions retained.

### Gate D — Measurement readiness
- source/campaign registry exists;
- store-native acquisition evidence is accessible;
- first-open/new-user definition known;
- activation hypothesis/event known;
- core-value retention observation window chosen;
- ad-serving metrics/UX guardrails known if ads launch with the app;
- UNKNOWN/SUPPRESSED/INCONCLUSIVE states supported.

### Gate E — Distribution readiness
- at least a small set of useful owned assets exists for high-priority problems;
- community permission state is checked before product-bearing posts;
- any selected social channel has an evidence-backed content job;
- support/feedback path exists;
- launch messages are traceable to the same product promise as the store page.

If a hard gate fails, fix the gate rather than compensating with more promotion.

## 3. Pre-launch sequence

### Phase 1 — Evidence packet
Create one compact launch evidence packet:
- target audience/territory;
- priority CEPs/problems;
- actual search/community/support evidence;
- positioning and proof hierarchy;
- competitor/substitute observations where verified;
- unresolved assumptions.

### Phase 2 — Store + owned-content seed
Prepare the default listing and a minimum useful content base before sending external traffic. A specialist user arriving from a technical/community discussion should find corroborating proof, not an empty company site.

Owned seed assets should solve recurring problems, not publish generic `coming soon` posts solely for volume.

### Phase 3 — Community permission and listening
Populate the permission ledger for intended communities. Before launch, useful participation can gather language, objections and missing proof. Product links remain permission-gated.

### Phase 4 — Measurement rehearsal
Verify source links/tokens, analytics events, store dashboards and ad telemetry where applicable. A launch should not be the first time the team discovers that source labels or activation events are unusable.

## 4. Pre-order / pre-registration is optional, not a default gate

### Apple
Current Apple documentation permits a new app to be published for pre-order in eligible countries/regions before download release. For a first-time release, the release date is set 2–180 days in the future. Customers can view the product page and pre-order; on release they are notified and the app can automatically download to eligible devices. App Analytics can report pre-orders by date, territory and source type.

Operational interpretation: Apple pre-order can be useful when there is a real pre-launch audience and a reason to collect intent before availability. It is not evidence of activation or retention and should not delay a ready specialist utility merely to manufacture a launch event.

### Google Play
Current Google Play documentation permits pre-registration but imposes a 90-day campaign window per country/region and recommends testing before starting it. Google states that eligible pre-registered users receive a notification at production launch and eligible devices may auto-install. Summary reporting includes sign-ups and campaign status.

Operational interpretation: do not open Play pre-registration until product declarations/configuration and launch timing are sufficiently mature to satisfy the 90-day constraint. Pre-registration count is an intent signal, not retained-user evidence.

### Company admission rule
Use pre-order/pre-registration only if all are true:
1. a reachable pre-launch audience exists;
2. the wait period has a concrete content/community job;
3. launch timing is credible;
4. the mechanism adds measurable learning or captures otherwise-lost intent;
5. operating it does not consume more scarce labor than the likely learning/value.

Otherwise launch normally.

## 5. Release control is not the same across Apple and Google

Apple currently supports manual release or automatic release after approval/no earlier than a specified date. Apple phased release is for version updates and follows a seven-day 1%→2%→5%→10%→20%→50%→100% sequence; users can still manually download an update at any time. This is not a first-launch traffic-throttling mechanism.

Google Play staged rollouts likewise apply to app updates, not first publication. They expose an update only to the selected percentage of randomly selected eligible new/existing users and can be increased over time.

Company rule: **never describe update phased/staged rollout as a first-launch acquisition strategy.** Initial-launch sequencing must instead use readiness, territory/availability choices, pre-order/pre-registration where justified, and controlled channel activation.

## 6. Launch-day channel sequencing

Do not publish every channel simultaneously merely to create noise.

Recommended order for a small specialist app:
1. verify live store page and critical install/first-use path;
2. verify source measurement;
3. activate owned site/content handoffs;
4. publish in explicitly permitted owned/community surfaces where the post itself is useful;
5. activate admitted social surfaces selectively;
6. observe store→activation quality before broadening distribution;
7. expand only when the promise and first-use experience remain aligned.

Reason: sequential activation creates more interpretable evidence and limits the blast radius of broken onboarding, misleading creative or telemetry failures.

## 7. First-cohort learning contract

For each meaningful source cohort, ask:
- did relevant users reach the store/page?
- did native store response improve?
- did first opens/new users appear under the analytics definition?
- did users reach meaningful activation?
- what friction/expectation mismatch appeared?
- did users return on the product's natural workflow cadence?
- did ad-bearing use remain acceptable where monetization is active?

Do not celebrate a launch from downloads alone.

## 8. Promise-consistency audit

Before every major launch message, compare:
`community/social/content claim ↔ store claim ↔ screenshot proof ↔ onboarding/first-use reality ↔ monetization reality`

Failure modes:
- acquisition message promises broader capability than product;
- screenshot implies automatic workflow that requires substantial manual work;
- community answer sounds educational but hides ownership/product interest;
- `free` messaging hides disruptive monetization;
- launch geography implies regulatory applicability not validated for that audience.

Any material mismatch is a launch blocker or requires narrower copy.

## 9. No-paid-spend fallback ladder

If qualified acquisition is weak, do not jump immediately to paid acquisition. Diagnose in order:
1. **discoverability failure** — insufficient relevant impressions/search/community reach;
2. **intent mismatch** — wrong query/community/CEP;
3. **store/proof failure** — relevant traffic but weak store response;
4. **activation failure** — installs but no first meaningful value;
5. **retention failure** — activation without recurring value;
6. **distribution durability failure** — one-off launch traffic with no evergreen path.

Cash-light responses can include better intent targeting, stronger proof, improved owned content/internal links, permitted community answers, store creative experiments and product/onboarding fixes. More traffic is not the default remedy for downstream failure.

## 10. Launch-week and post-launch cadence

### T0–T+2 days: integrity
Prioritize crashes, broken store links, measurement gaps, severe promise mismatch and support signals. Avoid overinterpreting tiny conversion/retention samples.

### T+3–T+14: qualification
Compare source quality and activation. Capture user language/friction. Revise misleading creative or onboarding. Do not optimize ad pressure from tiny early revenue samples.

### T+2–T+6 weeks: recurrence
Observe natural core-value return cadence, content/search discovery, community learning and sustainable ad-bearing use. Promote repeatable channels over launch spikes.

### Later: evergreen system
Maintain content, store experiments, community permission records, selective social and product improvements as ongoing distribution assets. A launch is successful when acquisition no longer depends on launch-day posting.

Windows are diagnostic defaults, not universal statistical thresholds; product cadence and sample size govern interpretation.

## 11. MintTap application hypotheses

Potential launch sequence, subject to live validation:
- prioritize YieldMax-specific problem/CEP proof rather than generic portfolio-tracker claims;
- seed high-value explanatory assets around validated distribution/ROC/split/cost-basis problems;
- use r/MintTapforYieldMax as an owned community surface while maintaining contribution quality;
- external YieldMax communities remain permission-gated;
- route distinct proven intents to appropriate store treatment only when traffic supports segmentation;
- validate activation and core-value return before increasing ad pressure.

Financial/YMYL content requires source/provenance/update discipline established in research 015.

## 12. LogMate application hypotheses

Potential sequence, subject to launch geography and product validation:
- establish professional-pilot segment/geography and regulatory boundaries first;
- seed migration/import/backup/data-ownership workflow content where evidence supports it;
- use pilot communities for learning and permitted contribution, not broad unsolicited promotion;
- do not imply universal regulatory compliance without jurisdiction-specific primary-source validation;
- measure first usable logbook establishment and natural workflow recurrence rather than generic DAU.

## 13. Reusable launch checklist schema

Every future app should carry a launch record with:
- product/version;
- territory/segment;
- launch date/window;
- audience evidence;
- priority CEPs;
- positioning/proof statement;
- store listing IDs/variants;
- source registry entries;
- owned seed assets;
- community permission records;
- admitted social surfaces;
- activation hypothesis;
- retention cadence hypothesis;
- monetization treatment/guardrails;
- rollback triggers;
- first-cohort observations;
- decisions and unresolved unknowns.

This record is evidence, not ceremony.

## 15. Retained expert judgment

For specialist apps with little/no paid acquisition, launch advantage comes less from concentrating attention into one day and more from **reducing uncertainty before distribution, preserving attribution/learning, and creating durable discovery assets**.

A useful launch therefore maximizes:
`qualified first cohorts + learning quality + durable distribution`

not:
`launch-day impressions + raw downloads`.
