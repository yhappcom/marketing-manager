# 095 — Apple Editorial Featuring as Earned Distribution

Date: 2026-09-18

## Canonical principle

**Nominate a real product moment, not a marketing wish. Editorial reach is earned distribution, not guaranteed inventory.**

## Why this matters

For a zero-paid-media niche app company, Apple editorial featuring is one of the few first-party distribution opportunities that can create incremental discovery without buying impressions. It is therefore worth operationalizing, but not treating as a forecastable acquisition channel.

Apple's current official guidance says developers can submit Featuring Nominations in App Store Connect for an app launch, significant app enhancement, or new content. Apple's editorial team considers nominations; submission does not guarantee featuring. Apple states that featuring is localized and regionally curated and may appear through stories, collections, App/Game of the Day, lists, personalized recommendations, Editors' Choice and other placements.

## Evidence from Apple

Current official Apple guidance establishes:

- indie developers and established developers can both be considered;
- nomination types include app launch, app enhancement, and new content;
- Apple recommends at least two weeks' notice on its general featuring page and says up to three months in advance for wider consideration;
- the App Store Connect nomination-template reference says to finalize and submit at least three weeks in advance;
- Apple editorial guidance also notes that more elaborate stories can require substantially longer planning;
- nominations can specify relevant countries/regions and localizations;
- up to five supplemental URLs can support a nomination, including documents, art assets, and public TestFlight links;
- helpful details can include accessibility, inclusivity, priority, and unique aspects of the app/team;
- Apple explicitly considers UX, UI design, innovation, uniqueness, accessibility, localization, product-page quality, ratings and reviews when considering featuring.

Operationally, use **≥3 weeks as the normal minimum planning floor**, while submitting materially earlier when a launch/update date is known. The apparent two-week/three-week wording across Apple pages should not be collapsed into a false exact deadline; three weeks is the safer operational floor, and broad/story consideration benefits from longer lead time.

## Editorial Readiness Gate: P0–P5

### P0 — fabricated moment
A nomination exists mainly because marketing wants exposure. No noteworthy shipped or imminent product change exists, or the nomination exaggerates the significance of a routine release.

**Action:** do not submit.

### P1 — real moment, weak product evidence
A genuine launch/update/content moment exists, but product quality, Store evidence, trust parity, specialist scope, or first-value semantics remain materially unresolved.

**Action:** fix the product/evidence gap before seeking amplification.

### P2 — credible moment, generic story
The product change is real and useful, but the nomination is essentially release notes or generic claims such as “best,” “easy,” or “innovative.” It does not explain who has a difficult job, what changed, and why the implementation is distinctive.

**Action:** do not spend editorial attention on generic copy.

### P3 — nomination-ready
A real launch/significant enhancement/new-content moment exists; claims are substantiated; relevant locale/storefront is production-ready; screenshots/product page/trust surfaces are current; and the nomination explains a concrete user problem, product change, and evidence.

**Action:** eligible for intentional nomination.

### P4 — editorial-ready package
P3 plus a concise editorial narrative, correct release timing, territory/localization selection, useful supplemental materials, accessibility/inclusivity facts where genuine, public TestFlight/demo evidence when appropriate, and an owner who can respond promptly to Apple asset/editorial requests.

### P5 — reusable earned-distribution system
Across multiple releases, the company reliably identifies legitimate product moments early, prepares evidence without delaying shipping, submits with adequate lead time, records outcome/placement/storefront, and evaluates downstream qualified acquisition and retained useful use rather than celebrating the feature badge itself.

## The nomination story model

Use:

`specialist problem → why existing workflow is difficult → concrete shipped change → distinctive implementation → who benefits → evidence → relevant geography/localization → release date`

Do not use:

`we launched → we are innovative → please feature us`

Editorial narrative is not permission to overclaim. Existing claim, localization, trust, review, and specialist-boundary contracts remain controlling.

## MintTap application

Potential future P3 moments are not every dividend-data refresh or minor UI release. Candidate moments should be materially user-relevant, such as a substantial new workflow that solves a difficult YieldMax portfolio-management problem and is production-valid across Store copy, screenshots, calculations, privacy/support surfaces, and first-value behavior.

A tax/ROC-related enhancement is nomination-worthy only if its wording accurately distinguishes tracking/recording support from tax advice or jurisdictional tax determination. Editorial ambition cannot widen the supported claim boundary.

For a US-focused YieldMax story, US storefront relevance can be explicit. Korean localization or KRW support should be mentioned only where semantic parity has passed the localization/trust gates.

## LogMate application

A first public launch could become a strong editorial moment because a niche professional workflow can be inherently distinctive, but only after production-valid manual entry/persistence, totals/aggregation, backup/offline semantics, Store trust surfaces, and professional/jurisdictional boundaries are validated.

The story should be the pilot's workflow problem and the product solution, not an unsupported implication of authority approval or regulatory compliance.

## Zero-cost operating calendar

At roadmap planning, flag possible editorial moments rather than inventing them after release.

- T-12 to T-8 weeks: identify major launch/update story; confirm supported claims and target storefronts.
- T-8 to T-4 weeks: assemble evidence, screenshots/demo/TestFlight, accessibility/localization facts, and editorial narrative.
- At least T-3 weeks: normal company submission floor for a planned nomination.
- Pre-release: update nomination if plans materially change; keep Store assets/trust surfaces synchronized.
- Post-selection: record actual placement/storefront/date; use Apple's provided promotional assets when available; measure downstream qualified acquisition and useful return.
- Post-non-selection: do not resubmit the same unchanged marketing wish. Wait for another genuine product moment or materially stronger evidence.

## Measurement

Featuring is not a scalable paid-media analogue. Maintain an earned-distribution ledger:

`nomination_id → product moment → P-class → submitted_at → release_at → storefront/localization → evidence URLs → outcome → placement → exposure window → Store acquisition delta → first value → useful return → retained-use revenue`

A feature badge, impressions, or download spike alone does not establish P5. A broad editorial spike that produces weak first value can be less useful than a smaller specialist-aligned placement.

## Company rule

1. Do not manufacture release scope merely to create a featuring nomination.
2. Do not nominate routine maintenance as a significant enhancement.
3. Do not delay important bug/security/trust fixes to create a marketing moment.
4. Submit early enough for editorial planning; use three weeks as the normal minimum operational floor and longer for major launch/story ambitions.
5. Treat nomination as free optional upside, never as a launch dependency or forecasted acquisition guarantee.
6. Reuse existing validated evidence; do not create unsupported editorial claims.
7. Measure post-feature quality through first value/useful return, not feature status alone.
