# Research 243 — Ad-request supply, delivery diagnostics, and safe mediation optimization

Date: 2026-09-24
Status: Canonical addition
Gate: FC0–FC5

## Why this research exists

Research 241 established seller/domain authorization and app readiness. Research 242 established impression-level paid-event measurement and protected, non-intrusive inventory. The next operational failure mode is to see weak ad revenue and jump directly to adding networks, raising ad frequency, or changing floors without identifying where the delivery chain is actually losing value.

For a sparse niche app, that is especially dangerous: small traffic makes revenue noisy, extra SDKs increase operational/privacy surface, and additional requests or placements can damage UX without fixing the real bottleneck.

## Authoritative findings

### 1. Ad delivery is a chain, not one metric

AdMob/Google Mobile Ads exposes distinct request/response stages. A loaded ad has `ResponseInfo`; adapter-level response information identifies the ad source, error state and latency. The adapter list follows mediation-waterfall order and the loaded-adapter response identifies the source that actually loaded the ad. Failed loads can also expose response information for diagnosis.

Therefore weak monetization must be localized before intervention:

`eligible opportunity → ad request → source participation → fill/load → eligible display opportunity → impression → paid event → reconciled revenue`

A failure at one stage cannot be safely treated as a failure at another.

### 2. Mediation diagnostics should precede mediation expansion

Google's Ad Inspector can inspect real-time test requests, waterfall execution, bidding results, adapter initialization, source errors and latency. It can distinguish a winning source, a source with an issue, a source that returned no ad/did not bid, and a source that submitted a losing bid. It also supports single-ad-source testing and privacy-signal inspection on supported SDKs.

This means “add another ad network” is not a default remedy for low revenue. First determine whether the existing stack has integration, adapter, signal, latency, no-fill, display-opportunity, privacy, or inventory problems.

### 3. Bidding and waterfall are different mechanisms inside one mediation decision

Google documents that bidding sources compete in an auction and the winning bid can be placed into the mediation waterfall according to eCPM value; existing waterfall sources can continue to participate. A bidder that submits a losing bid is not simply evidence of a broken integration. Likewise, `no ad returned` can have multiple causes and should not be converted into a blanket conclusion that the network is poor.

The correct unit of diagnosis is the request/source outcome plus error/latency/context, not a network name in isolation.

### 4. Revenue optimization must not manufacture ad demand

Research 242's paid-event evidence remains downstream of product-created eligible opportunities. The app must not create extra navigation steps, artificial pauses, forced refreshes, or unnecessary ad requests merely to increase auction opportunities. The monetization system optimizes *eligible inventory generated by genuine product use*; it does not redesign the product to manufacture inventory.

For specialist apps, the optimization objective is therefore not maximum requests or maximum fill. It is maximum sustainable reconciled revenue subject to protected workflows, privacy/consent correctness, reliability, and repeated specialist value.

### 5. Sparse traffic changes the decision rule

Small niche cohorts make country, format, source and day-level monetization data unstable. A short-lived eCPM, fill, latency or source-win change should not trigger configuration churn. Segment only when the segment has a real decision purpose and enough observations to distinguish a persistent bottleneck from traffic mix/noise.

Do not infer:

- low revenue → insufficient ad density;
- low eCPM → add networks;
- no-fill → broken SDK;
- losing bid → bad network;
- high fill → good UX or good monetization;
- more requests → more sustainable revenue;
- a test-device result → production economics;
- one country's short-window result → global configuration rule.

## FC0–FC5 — Ad Delivery & Safe Mediation Optimization Integrity Gate

### FC0 — Eligible-opportunity identity
Define the legitimate product moment and protected-surface status before examining ad economics. No optimization is allowed to manufacture additional product friction or ad opportunities.

### FC1 — Delivery-stage identity
Locate the loss: opportunity, request, source participation, load/fill, display, impression, paid event, or aggregate reconciliation. Preserve format, ad unit, app version, platform and relevant geography/time cohort.

### FC2 — Integration/diagnostic integrity
Use test devices and Ad Inspector/response information to inspect adapter initialization, waterfall/bidding execution, errors, latency, no-ad outcomes and privacy signals. Do not diagnose production supply from aggregate revenue alone.

### FC3 — Supply/mediation integrity
Only change mediation or source configuration when evidence identifies a persistent supply/competition problem that the change can plausibly address. Adding SDKs/sources incurs privacy, maintenance, binary-size and reliability costs and therefore needs an explicit expected benefit.

### FC4 — Sparse-evidence/change-control integrity
Define the decision metric, cohort, observation window and rollback condition before changing configuration. Avoid repeated tuning on small samples. One material variable per controlled change where practical; preserve a pre-change baseline.

### FC5 — Sustainable-value decision
Approve a monetization change only if reconciled revenue improves without violating protected workflows, privacy/consent, technical reliability, accidental-click safeguards or repeated specialist value. Revenue per eligible opportunity/user/session can inform the decision; request count, CTR or eCPM alone cannot.

## MintTap application

1. Reconstruct `ad unit → surface → format → eligible opportunity → request/load/impression/paid-event` before changing mediation.
2. Keep portfolio setup/editing and financially sensitive ROC/tax interpretation protected.
3. For each currently monetized secondary surface, diagnose request-to-impression losses with response information/Ad Inspector on test devices before adding networks or changing exposure.
4. Do not add mediation partners merely because MintTap's niche traffic produces low absolute revenue. Partner/SDK expansion needs evidence of a persistent addressable supply problem plus a privacy/maintenance review.
5. Compare sustainable revenue at the surface/cohort level with repeated specialist-value signals; do not optimize around CTR.

## LogMate application

LogMate remains pre-release and should not design its product around mediation. If advertising is introduced later:

- Home and critical logging/import/export/sync/totals/validation/recovery workflows remain protected;
- first establish a small set of legitimate secondary-surface opportunities;
- validate base AdMob delivery and paid-event telemetry before introducing mediation complexity;
- only add another source when production evidence demonstrates a persistent addressable supply gap and the added SDK/privacy/maintenance burden is justified.

This prevents a niche professional utility from acquiring a large ad-tech stack before it has enough traffic to prove the stack creates incremental sustainable value.

## Reusable company operating contract

For future niche apps:

`protected-surface map → eligible opportunity → request → response/source diagnostics → display/impression → paid event + precision → aggregate reconciliation → persistent bottleneck classification → smallest justified configuration change → controlled observation → sustainable-value decision`

The company default is **diagnose before expanding**.

## New canonical prohibitions

- Do not use ad density to compensate for unresolved delivery/supply problems.
- Do not add mediation partners without an identified addressable bottleneck.
- Do not interpret a losing bid as an integration failure.
- Do not interpret no-fill without request/source/error context.
- Do not optimize mediation from CTR.
- Do not churn floors/sources/configuration on sparse short-window data.
- Do not allow monetization configuration to redefine protected product workflows.
