# Research 389 — Ad Interruption Budget

Validated: 2026-10-03

## Finding
For specialist utility apps, ad monetization should be governed by an interruption budget rather than maximizing impression opportunities.

Google Play's current Better Ads guidance prohibits unexpected full-screen interstitials that interrupt a user's intended action. AdMob guidance places interstitials at logical breaks, warns against repeated interstitials, and warns that banners near navigation or interactive elements increase accidental-click risk.

The important distinction is that a platform policy ceiling is not a recommended product target. A compliant placement can still be strategically poor if it damages task continuity, retention, or trust.

## IK0–IK9
specialist job/trust sensitivity → protected workflow → natural boundary → format eligibility → accidental-click guard → session interruption budget → cooldown/cap → failure fallback → retained-value plus reconciled-revenue measurement → KEEP / REDUCE / MOVE / REMOVE / HOLD-NO-SAFE-BOUNDARY.

## Operating rule
Protect data entry, calculations, import/export, authentication/onboarding, and professional reference workflows by default. Do not place a full-screen ad between a user command and the result the command is expected to produce.

Optimize retained specialist value multiplied by eligible monetization opportunities and realized revenue, subject to policy, privacy/consent, latency, traffic-quality, and trust constraints. Do not optimize impressions per session or eCPM alone.

## App application
MintTap: audit current ad triggers before increasing ad pressure. Treat active portfolio editing, calculations, distribution/ROC review, tax adjustment, and reconstruction workflows as protected by default.

LogMate: use a stricter default. Protect flight/multi-leg entry, import/migration, duplicate reconciliation, totals, certificate/export, and professional-reference workflows. Evaluate ads only at genuine post-task or passive boundaries and do not restrict normal use to manufacture ad exposure.
