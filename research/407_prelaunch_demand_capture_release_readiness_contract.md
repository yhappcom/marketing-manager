# Research 407 — Pre-Launch Demand Capture Is a Release-Readiness Contract

Date: 2026-10-03
Status: VALIDATED
Scope: zero-cash launch operations, Store pre-order/pre-registration, niche-app launch readiness, LogMate-first application

## Executive conclusion

For a specialist app, Apple pre-order and Google Play pre-registration should not be activated merely because the Store supports them. They convert pre-launch attention into a platform-managed future delivery promise. The admission criterion is release confidence plus a truthful, production-near Store promise, not desire to accumulate a large waitlist.

specialist problem evidence → release-readiness gate → truthful Store promise → permissioned pre-launch route → pre-order/pre-registration → release delivery → first specialist value → repeated specialist value

A signup is an intermediate commitment signal. It is not an install, activation, retained user, or proof of product-market fit.

## Apple App Store — validated platform facts

Apple currently permits free and paid apps to be offered for pre-order in territories where the app has not yet been released. For a first release, the release date must be 2–180 days in the future. Once an app has been released in a territory, that territory cannot subsequently be converted back to pre-order.

Customers can view the product page before release. At release, customers are notified and the app can automatically download to eligible devices under Apple's delivery conditions.

Apple supports promoting a pre-order through owned channels such as a website, mailing list, and social media using a direct product-page link. App Analytics can break pre-orders down by date, territory, and source type; Sales and Trends can expose purchased and cancelled pre-orders.

If a release date changes, Apple states that customers are not automatically notified of the date change or removal of the pre-order; the developer is responsible for communicating the change. This creates a trust obligation for niche launches.

Apple also permits custom product pages for apps available for pre-order. Audience-specific pre-launch routing is therefore technically possible, but remains subject to the repository's claim-truth and sparse-traffic rules.

Primary sources: Apple Developer, “Publish for pre-order”; Apple Developer, “Pre-orders”; Apple Developer, “Custom Product Pages”. Verified 2026-10-03.

## Google Play — validated platform facts

Google strongly recommends testing on a test track before beginning pre-registration and recommends starting only when declarations and configurations are close to the intended production version.

A Play pre-registration campaign can run for at most 90 days. Google's current documentation states that if launch does not occur within 90 days in participating countries, the campaign can be terminated and future pre-registration availability can be affected.

Google permits at most two apps or games to be available for pre-registration at one time. Countries/regions can be added after campaign start; the 90-day clock for a country begins when pre-registration is first enabled there.

At production launch, pre-registered users receive a Play Store notification and eligible devices may have the app automatically installed. Play Console exposes sign-ups, supported devices, country/region count, and campaign status.

Primary source: Google Play Console Help, “Build awareness for your apps with pre-registration”. Verified 2026-10-03.

## Synthesis — pre-launch Store surfaces create commitment asymmetry

Before activation, a delay is mostly an internal schedule problem. After pre-order/pre-registration begins, a delay affects users who have made an explicit future-use commitment. Apple places communication responsibility on the developer when the pre-order date changes. Google imposes a hard operational window on pre-registration.

Therefore community problem discovery, pilot interviews, owned-reference publishing, Store claim validation, and test-track quality work can happen before platform pre-order/pre-registration. The Store mechanism should be the conversion layer near release, not the beginning of launch learning.

## JQ0–JQ9 — Pre-Launch Demand Capture Contract

JQ0 Specialist-problem evidence — confirm that the launch message maps to a recurring real job/problem rather than generic category interest.

JQ1 Release-readiness gate — do not activate a platform commitment until product, declarations, privacy/policy state, critical workflows, and release operations are sufficiently close to production.

JQ2 Claim-registry gate — every Store statement, screenshot and pre-launch message must describe functionality expected to exist at release with evidence, scope and invalidation owner.

JQ3 Platform-window gate — record the exact territory-specific Apple release window and Google Play 90-day clock before activation.

JQ4 Permissioned routing — use Store direct links only from channels where contribution/promotion is allowed. A pre-registration URL does not override community anti-promotion rules.

JQ5 Commitment-state measurement — keep page visit, pre-order/pre-registration, cancellation/unregistration where observable, launch delivery, install, first launch and first specialist value as separate states.

JQ6 Delay/change communication — maintain an owned communication path and update affected launch messaging if release timing materially changes.

JQ7 Launch-delivery verification — verify notification/automatic-delivery assumptions and actual production availability by territory. Do not equate sign-up count with delivered install count.

JQ8 Post-launch value closure — judge the pre-launch route by first and repeated specialist value after release, not by pre-registration volume alone.

JQ9 Decision — OPEN / HOLD-NOT-READY / ROUTE-TO-STORE / DELAY / LAUNCH / REPAIR-PROMISE / RETIRE-ROUTE.

## LogMate application

LogMate is the strongest current candidate because it is pre-launch and addresses professional workflows where a broken first release can damage trust disproportionately.

Do not activate Apple pre-order or Google Play pre-registration merely to collect pilots early. First verify the launch-critical chain around import/migration, duplicate handling, Previous Total continuity, multi-leg logging, export/certificate integrity, offline/device/PWA boundaries, and privacy/auth/sync claims that appear on the Store page.

Pilot-community research and useful native participation may precede Store pre-registration. Once release confidence is high enough, a direct pre-order/pre-registration route can convert already-earned interest into a platform-managed launch reminder/delivery mechanism.

Because the audience is narrow, raw pre-registration count is especially dangerous as a vanity metric. The useful question is whether the pre-launch cohort reaches first specialist value and returns for the real professional workflow after release.

## MintTap application

MintTap is already live, so first-release pre-registration is not the operative growth lever in already-released territories. Apple can support pre-order when expanding an existing app into a new territory that has not yet received the app, but that is a territory-launch mechanism, not a general reactivation tool.

MintTap's current zero-cost priorities remain Store intent routing, community/owned-reference evidence, activation quality and non-intrusive ad yield.

## Reusable niche-app rule

Use platform pre-launch commitment only when all five are true: a real specialist problem and launch audience are evidenced; the Store promise is truthful and release-scoped; critical first-value workflows are release-ready enough to justify a public commitment; platform deadline and territory mechanics are operationally owned; downstream activation and repeated-value measurement exist or are scheduled for launch.

Otherwise continue pre-launch learning without opening the Store commitment.
